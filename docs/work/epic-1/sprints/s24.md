**Track:** full · **Needs:** S23A · **Owners:** E03, E08

- **Goal.** An instance never takes more memory than its budget: a write that would
  exceed it is refused before it has any effect, and a child that reaches it stops
  applying and keeps its reads.
- **Contract.** The memory budget declared in S09 is enforced for the whole instance.
  `WriteRejected` names memory as the limit.
- **To read.** [Initial bounded settings](../../../design/milestone-1-engine-contract.md#initial-bounded-settings);
  [Q14 master memory pressure, child memory pressure](../../../decisions/write-admission.md);
  [index preparation, capacity reservation](../../../design/milestone-1-engine-contract.md#index-preparation-and-traversal).
- **Acceptance.** A write that cannot reserve its memory is refused before commit and
  does not wait for compaction; reads continue, and after memory is freed writes are
  accepted again. The budget counts the arena with its reserve, both tables of a growing
  primary index, secondary index nodes, the text table, the native scratch of reads if any exists, and the pages of root entries
  (prepared arena capacity has been counted since S09). A waiting write takes no new
  preparation reservation. Already owned batch input stays charged. S26 adds the
  retained outcomes of a MemoryOnly master. A failed reservation returns what it took,
  shown by the counters. On a child, a deterministic capacity failure while
  preparing full roots leaves no newly retained handles or arena blocks after
  rollback, verified by an independent tally for initial load and transaction
  replay. A child over its budget pauses application, keeps its reads and
  resumes when memory is free again; reloading after a long pause is S34. Nothing queued
  runs after its cancellation.
- **Measured.** Reported: peak against steady memory of one MemoryOnly node held at its
  budget under the churn trace; the share of the budget each counted part takes on the
  reference fixture. Also a write that alternates with indexed writes at the limit: the
  top-up of the standing reserve of S14, part 2 takes back what the give-back before a
  refusal freed, so such a write may go through the whole resolution every time; this is
  read, not measured, and it is measured here before anyone changes that rule (the
  planner, 20 November 2026).
- **Out of scope.** Budgets of the journal and of delivery history (S31, S33).

**Carried from the architect's review after S13 (18 November 2026).** Whether a text
entry is allocated live from the start, instead of prepared and then published, is
decided here with the charge of the text table to the budget: it changes what
`PreparedBytes` counts for a write that is later withdrawn, which is accounting and not
structure, so the refactoring sprint after S13 did not take it.

**Memory carried from S17.** Use the [S23 inventory](s23.md#retained-memory-inventory-for-s24) to account for retained capacity
in the instance budget, including publication scratch, batch claims, the child's key
set and connection buffer, and the text table at its actual capacity. S24 owns reservation
and rollback on refusal. The connection buffer's retention policy belongs to S33.

**Added by the planner on 24 November 2026, from the architect's review after S18.** S18 added managed room the budget does not count: the buffers of `BlockImport`
(about 145 KB an instance) and its starts, and the pieces `ExportedValues` keeps for a
member of a batch from its `Add` to its commit; the brief names each before the sprint
starts.

## Contract boundaries

The planner settled [Q31](../../open-questions.md#q31-native-bytes-that-no-counter-of-the-arena-shows)
and [Q33](../../open-questions.md#q33-what-an-application-sees-when-the-table-of-root-entries-is-full)
on 26 November 2026. Budget admission uses the actual allocated capacity of
each arena segment, including the empty reserve. Reserve the entire native
grant and its managed backing before allocating them. Unwind the reservation
if the allocator fails. Retagging a region, moving a slot among prepared, live
and retired states, and activating the reserve keep the same native charge.
Secondary nodes and native text blocks are parts of that charge. With the
standard 64 KiB region, a 3,328-byte class fits 19 slots and leaves 2,304 bytes.
Test 18, 19 and 20 slots. A separate allocator ledger must observe nonzero
grants, reserve reuse and physical returns and reconcile them with live,
prepared and retired slots, class slack, idle capacity and tails. Test the
next 4 MiB grant at one byte below, exactly at, and one byte above the
required budget after including the managed baseline and allocation peak.

Add `LatticeLimitEnum.RootSlots = 4`. A master write that needs another
lifetime and cannot address one reports `WriteRejected` with `RootSlots`.
If a slot can be addressed but its new page or directory cannot fit, the
result is `WriteRejected` with `Memory`. An existing free slot is used
without requiring new page capacity. Use a distinct internal result for
address exhaustion. A reusable slot held by an earlier write or reader is
subject to the bounded resolution in Q14 and O20. Permanent exhaustion
without a source of reusable slots gives no reason to wait for compaction.
Both resource refusals leave a batch's `OperationIndex` unset and preserve
all earlier data, revisions and position. Test both through a TCP-forwarded
write. Exercise 4,095 slots, 4,096 slots and the next insertion with one
real page in the reduced-address test. Independently confirm the production
limit of 524,287 pages and 2,147,479,552 slots. Hold a deleted slot with a
reader, cover reuse with unchanged byte charge, and prove that a slot freed
after generation `uint.MaxValue` stays out of the free list. Check the
stamped handle independently before and after ordinary reuse. Test the
separate page-byte reservation boundary while a new address is available.

Every address-bearing handle issued by the root table advances that slot's
generation, including a prepared handle whose write subsequently fails or is
cancelled. The first issued generation is 1. Withdrawing a never-published
tail page returns the page and any directory no remaining owner needs. The
table keeps only the exact generation information it needs to issue future
handles. This is charged root-table capacity with a continuing owner. Reserve
required backing before issuing the handle. Withdrawal after issue allocates
no history. Transfer earlier generations into a recreated page before giving
up its history, including entries that have not yet been issued again. A
slot released at its final generation remains retired.

Keep the 196,664-byte return of the original empty-tail case and distinguish
restoration of an intermediate directory from retention of a directory whose
new position is in use. Check a synchronous preparation failure and queued
cancellation, the two separate histories with generations (2, 1) and (1, 2),
repeated withdrawal and recreation, and a new neighboring slot at
generation 1. A previously issued handle must fail both current-handle and
entry lookup checks after the same slot is issued again. Check byte refusal
before a history backing grows and local exhaustion at the final generation.
Measure the fixed contribution on an empty instance and the actual acquired
and released backing for a nontrivial sparse history.

Before Ready, controlled failure to hold the state ends open with
`Unavailable`. Check the announced primary-index capacity with the least
root bytes before installing data, and check actual grants during the load.
After Ready, keep the installed data readable at the same position and pause
at the first blocked change. Record the internal reason separately from
position and distinguish byte and slot pressure. The first blocked change is
unacknowledged and retries before any later change. Retry with bounded,
cancellable waits that allow close, local reads and maintenance to proceed.
A reusable native or root slot can restore progress without a lower byte
charge. Arbitrary allocation or publication failures retain their existing
failure handling. The public pressure reason and known master progress
belong to [S39](s39.md#s39--diagnostics).

Test replication pressure both while receiving a transaction and while
preparing an assembled transaction. A single-frame body borrows the current
receive buffer. An assembled multi-frame body borrows its charged pieces.
Keep either body valid until the transaction finishes. During assembly,
validate the frame and reserve its new backing capacity before irrevocably
consuming its bytes. A refused first, intermediate or last frame can be
retried once capacity permits without consuming its bytes twice. Keep only
the current bounded receive buffer and the already charged pieces while
paused. Distinguish a valid announcement beyond available capacity from a
malformed frame. Compare a new allocation with reuse of pieces retained from
a preceding message. Use a real TCP child to test the received-byte boundary
and the applied-position boundary, including cancellation and close in both,
unchanged reads and indexes, and once-only ordered completion. A write reply
interleaved with transaction frames keeps its actual outcome. Processing
cannot advance past the blocked frame to manufacture or acknowledge a result.
The master does not wait for the child to commit a write.

A Ready child pauses on the same live connection and retries in order when
capacity permits. A persistent shortage can keep it paused at that frame or
transaction. An observed end of the connection or close uses the existing
cursor cleanup. A quiet peer can become unreachable while the child holds a
frame. S33 adds the retention limit and slow peer policy. S34 adds reconnect
and reload. Until then a stalled cursor can retain a growing delivery tail
on the master. The finite TCP scenarios verify ownership and progress.

Account independently for the instance budget charge, logical
`PendingDeliveryBytes` and physical delivery tail capacity. The transaction
encoder writes directly into the tail. Its physical capacity is a separate
reported figure outside S24 admission from allocation until release. Include
blocks from Begin before commit, blocks retained for a reader, and the spare.
Count each physical block once. Commit changes the logical pending bytes.
Charge actual preparation, publication, independent batch payload pieces,
network buffer and child assembly capacities to the instance budget.
Test that finite later master writes complete during a real pause, that
physical delivery grows separately from budget admission, and that the
live connection resumes from the first blocked sequence. Also verify that a
connection end releases its cursor while other cursors may still hold blocks.
Test an interleaved reply before and after the blocked frame. A known
committed outcome and its local position wait keep their existing semantics.
See X6 in the [exceptions register](../../exceptions.md) for the wait on an unknown
fully sent outcome.

A batch's adding methods reserve local memory for their snapshot and
workspace on either role before sending or queueing anything. A failed
reservation synchronously reports `WriteRejected` with `Memory` and no
`OperationIndex`. Keep the current order of input, duplicate, submitted
and closed checks for full values and partial updates. Keep prior members,
count, claimed identities and an unconsumed update when the reservation
fails. The caller can try the same addition again. Budget the temporary
export and its final single-buffer or multi-piece copy while both are
owned. Include the real list and identity-set replacement peaks. The
caller's original input remains outside the charge.

On an open instance, an addition stops at the first allocation for its
snapshot or workspace whose reservation cannot fit. It reports synchronous
`WriteRejected` with `Memory` and no `OperationIndex`. Argument, binding
and schema errors already found retain their place in the order of errors.
Later getters and collection elements remain unread. With sufficient
capacity, preserve the established validation order and failures for
full values and updates, including duplicate, submitted and closed
checks. A refused allocation can precede a duplicate key or a submitted
batch check when the established order validates the snapshot first.
The update builder's own submitted, binding and empty checks still govern
before its snapshot is built. A closed instance keeps its established
failure order. Use
the actual capacity of the next allocation and reserve it before taking
it. As the caller's values are visited, each getter is read once.

Cover an open master and child with the same record that contains a key
and a large List, followed by a required field with no value. With
sufficient capacity this reports `ValidationFailed`. Before the later
field, a genuine snapshot allocation boundary reports `Memory`. Also
check an earlier error at that same capacity, exact getter calls on a
bound class, and the owned nested snapshot of `AddUpdate`. Preserve
existing members, identities, values and an unconsumed update across
both failures. The independent inventory must see the temporary charge
returned. After capacity returns, a repeated addition reaches the
original late validation error.

Submitting the completed batch transfers ownership of its charged input.
Waiting adds no new preparation reservation. Commit can independently
be refused for execution capacity. End each charge when the library loses
its last owner, including after cancellation or refusal. The ledger must
allow collection of an abandoned builder and eventually return its charge
without requiring explicit disposal. Test two batches competing for the
remaining budget, a preserved update and prior members after refusal,
successful retry after release, a held builder after Commit, and a local
child refusal that sends no request. Compare the two snapshot storage
shapes and the temporary and retained capacities independently.

Undo all unused reservations and all capacity owned solely by a failed or
cancelled preparation, including new managed backing and newly granted native
segments. Drop abandoned managed references as part of rollback. The GC
decides when unreachable memory is collected. Previously owned and still
reusable capacity remains charged. Reconcile the actual owners independently
when earlier retirement or other work changes the total. Exercise real
allocation failure and deterministic budget refusal, a failure after earlier
members in a large batch, and cancellation before dispatch and while
resolving pressure. On a TCP child, check a pre-Ready failure and a Ready
pause with nonzero ledgers for native grants, root handles and prepared
blocks. Reach the full-root and oversized workspace paths corrected by S23D
and S23F. Confirm unchanged local results and position at the pause, success
of compatible local reads, no acknowledgement past the pause, and ordered,
once-only resume after memory or a usable slot returns.

## Internal observation

The tests and measurement program use
`EngineDatabase.InspectBudget(Action<string, object>? observeBacking = null)`
to read `BudgetCapacitySample` with `ChargedBytes`, `PeakBytes`, `NativeBytes`
and `DeliveryCapacityBytes`. A synchronized inventory visits each actual
managed backing at most once. The tests independently walk the owners for
the holder under test and size its backing. The admission sample is also
readable on the allocating thread before a physical native grant. There
`ChargedBytes` includes outstanding native and managed reservations, and
`NativeBytes` still means physical bytes. The visitor can be omitted in
that case.

`EngineDatabase.HoldBudgetBacking(int length)` provides a real charged
array with an idempotent `IDisposable` release to align independent test
events. Actual native and root slots provide separate reuse and
reclamation controls. `EngineTestInputs.RootPageLimit` limits the count of
real 4,096-entry pages for tests. A null option uses production geometry.

`EngineTestInputs.ObserveChildPressure` takes an `Action<ChildPressureSample>`
with `Sequence`, `Limit`, `DuringReceive` and `ReceivedFrames`. Notify after
rollback at the actual Ready pause outside the writes lock. The frame count
excludes the refused frame. `BeforeChildTransactionFrame` is a test-only
`Action<ulong, int>` outside the writes lock before each attempted
admission of a valid frame, including retries. `BeforeChildPreparationMember`
is a test-only `Action<uint>` under the writes lock before each attempt to
prepare a replay effect. Count attempts from these points. The tests use the
existing maintenance, growth, publication and allocator controls where
those already expose the required distinction.

`EngineTestInputs.ObserveMasterRequestFrame` is a test-only
`Action<RequestReceiver>` on the master's receiving task. It runs once
after accepting a forwarded request frame and before giving a completed
body to the write path. It exposes the actual request pieces, import
workspace and recorded reply owner for independent inventory. The master
sender calls the test-only `EngineTestInputs.BeforeMasterReplyDrain` of
type `Func<CancellationToken, ValueTask>` once per nonempty drain, before
taking the first reply. It runs outside the writes and reply queue locks
and receives the connection cancellation token.

## Source study

The independent study checked each licence at the source commit before
reading the implementation. Lattice uses the following ideas without
adapting source code.

- Apache DataFusion's greedy pool uses a single atomic check and update for
  reserve. An unsuccessful reservation leaves the balance unchanged. Its
  reservations can transfer existing charges and return them at the end of
  ownership. Shared updates cost synchronization and require an owner for
  every charge. Use one synchronized admission balance and charge each
  backing store for its owned lifetime.
  [Source](https://github.com/apache/datafusion/blob/273d37a5968571900bfe9efa1ee89f97914da118/datafusion/execution/src/memory_pool/pool.rs#L81-L103) ·
  [Reservation transfer](https://github.com/apache/datafusion/blob/273d37a5968571900bfe9efa1ee89f97914da118/datafusion/execution/src/memory_pool/mod.rs#L430-L475) ·
  [Apache-2.0 licence](https://github.com/apache/datafusion/blob/273d37a5968571900bfe9efa1ee89f97914da118/LICENSE.txt)
- DuckDB reserves the aligned buffer allocation before acquiring it, can
  reuse an equal-size buffer, and excludes reader-pinned blocks from
  unloading. Eviction costs walks, synchronization and possible spill.
  Reuse already charged capacity and retain its charge until safe release.
  Exact admission and replacement peaks require Lattice to track both owned
  allocations. DuckDB's cached adjustments and spill policy do not carry
  those guarantees.
  [Reservation and reuse](https://github.com/duckdb/duckdb/blob/7c039464e452ddc3330e2691d3fa6d305521d09b/src/storage/standard_buffer_manager.cpp#L158-L171) ·
  [Pinned blocks](https://github.com/duckdb/duckdb/blob/7c039464e452ddc3330e2691d3fa6d305521d09b/src/storage/buffer/block_handle.cpp#L160-L197) ·
  [Cached adjustments](https://github.com/duckdb/duckdb/blob/7c039464e452ddc3330e2691d3fa6d305521d09b/src/include/duckdb/storage/buffer/buffer_pool.hpp#L121-L139) ·
  [MIT licence](https://github.com/duckdb/duckdb/blob/7c039464e452ddc3330e2691d3fa6d305521d09b/LICENSE)
- etcd Raft limits outstanding bytes of accepted entries and separately
  advances applied progress on completion. This costs separate cursors,
  capacity bookkeeping and a shorter pipeline under pressure. Keep the
  ordered apply boundary and separate completion fence. Raft's encoded-byte
  limit covers outstanding log work. Lattice also needs its own physical
  resident and preparation reservations and safe rollback over TCP.
  [Ordered acceptance](https://github.com/etcd-io/raft/blob/f35a02212416d045c574e30aa59d4b07c4b8d732/log.go#L218-L241) ·
  [Completion](https://github.com/etcd-io/raft/blob/f35a02212416d045c574e30aa59d4b07c4b8d732/log.go#L330-L363) ·
  [Apache-2.0 licence](https://github.com/etcd-io/raft/blob/f35a02212416d045c574e30aa59d4b07c4b8d732/LICENSE)

Use the fixed-volume reference churn from S23 (seed 6035, tape SHA-256

Use the local `development-5` manifest when its machine and affinity checks

## Implementation evidence

The physical accounting tests use native allocator grants and independently
sized managed owners. One 4 MiB arena grant counts in full even if its last
region cannot serve another slot of the active class. Root address
exhaustion uses a reduced physical table in tests and separately checks its
production representable range. The tests exercise bytes on either side of
admission, a previously reusable slot at the same byte count and a generation
that is permanently exhausted.

At late cancellation, reuse the existing outcome of a prepared write.
Each shared owner checks what the remaining writes or protected readers
actually hold. Immediate failed preparation can restore its previous
backing within the preparation turn. Removing a queued write can only
restore backing that a later preparation did not need. A reader may
continue to own a published generation through its epoch. Tests compare
the live backing with the equivalent surviving work and exercise outcomes
with another prepared writer and a held reader. The separate standing
reserve stays charged.

A segment address directory that grew from four to eight positions can
return 64 B of exclusive managed backing while its original segment
addresses remain readable. A new address already used by another
preparation holds the directory. Withdrawing an exclusively prepared
root page returns 196,664 B of page backing. A previously started TCP
capture remains bounded by its published fence and can finish after the
withdrawal. Reissuing a slot takes its exact next generation after page
trim; a new neighbor starts at generation 1. In the independent sparse
history probe, 272 B of continuing table metadata held the different
generations, and the charge returned when ownership transferred into the
page. The two distinct histories produced generations (3, 2) and (2, 3)
on reuse. A prepared handle from either withdrawn history was already
stale before that reuse.

A 4,096-command sparse preparation held 229,400 B of command array and
440 B of table, buckets and entries. Refusal and cancellation returned
their capacity without publication. Success kept charge through encoding
and released the prepared owners after publication. Separate TCP cases
hold actual decoded operand identities and reply parts through their owned
lifetime, and check refusal and resumption without advancing the applied
position while blocked.

The independent breaker ran 18 cases on runtime revision
`bb793b52eeed1ea47c187ec030f78fe639c19318`. The old failures in directory,
index, collection, and workspace withdrawal all passed. With a protected
reader and a later prepared write, the cancellation lane and its equivalent
control held 2,832 B of index tables before and after publication and
1,584 B after the reader left. A real TCP child stopped first at receive
for Memory and then at application for RootSlots on the same transaction.
Its applied position moved only after the slot became reusable.

On the same revision a separate TCP probe held 8,192 decoded text operands
of a forwarded write, totalling 262,064 B by measured object identities.
All appeared in the charged owner inventory, and the two nodes subsequently
published the same transaction. A split UTF-8 operand now decodes directly
into the reserved destination string after validation. The old runtime
rented an array of 65,536 chars in the distinguishing test. The wire format
uses eight bytes for DateTimeOffset. Its managed box takes 32 B, verified
by object size and matched by the new reservation on both the receive and
replay paths.

The early debug capacity probe once found a −48 B difference (charge minus
independent size). Strict repeated runs at the same sent boundary did
not reproduce it. A separate −200 B effect was reproduced by the test
sizer when a reflection cache was recreated in .NET 10.0.15. The final
sizer reads the existing backing and checks its alignment independently.
That correction explains the −200 B observations only. Raw probe runs
stay outside Git.

The formal tests-first review took two rounds. The later snapshot contract
and test refinements had their own acceptance reviews. The scoped runtime
fixes were rechecked before one consolidated independent review of the
accepted implementation boundaries. It found no open P1 or P2 on the exact
runtime revision above. The coordinator built the clean revision in Debug
and Release, verified format and ran all 18 samples. Both complete test
suites reported 4,818 passes, 129 expected skips and no failures.
