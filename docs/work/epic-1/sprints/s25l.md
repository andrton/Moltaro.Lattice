# S25L · One lifecycle of the instance

**Track:** full, critical (concurrency) · **Needs:** S25 · **Before:** S26, S27

**Why here.** The architect's review of 27 November 2026 found the lifecycle of an
instance kept as several separate marks — of the database, of the read epochs, of the
close barrier, of the position clock, of the write coordinator and of the budget — with
the precedence of Closed, Faulted, Lost, cancellation and timeout decided at each call
site (`PositionClock`, `SetOperations`, `Forwarder`, `EngineDatabase`). The fixes of
S22C, S23, S23B and S23C to S23F were each one operation reading one mark after another
had changed. S26 to S29 add the states of durability; they are to be added to one place.

- **Goal.** The lifecycle of an instance is one state owned by the database, and every
  operation that can end in more than one of Closed, Faulted, Lost, cancelled and timed
  out decides its outcome by one rule of precedence.
- **Contract.** None public. The internal lifecycle type and the precedence table are
  written into this brief by the planner after the study, before step 1, and reviewed as
  a document.
- **Study.** Bounded, read-only: the precedence each call site applies today, with the
  test that holds it; what the decision records say
  ([session consistency](../../../decisions/session-consistency.md),
  [write acknowledgment](../../../decisions/write-acknowledgment.md),
  [replication and hosting](../../../decisions/replication-and-hosting.md)); and how one
  or two established systems with an owned lifecycle order the close, a fault and the
  cancellation of pending operations.
- **Acceptance.**
  - The precedence table is stated with one test per pair of competing outcomes for each
    kind of wait: a position wait, a submitted write, a session read, a write forwarded
    by a child. Where two call sites decide the
    same pair differently today, the table follows the decision records and the
    difference is listed for the planner before implementation; if no record decides it,
    it is a question for the owner.
  - The position clock, the read epochs, the write coordinator and the budget read the
    database's state and keep no lifecycle mark of their own; a standalone clock for
    tests is built on the same state.
  - Each operation enters and leaves the close barrier through one scope.
  - All existing tests pass unchanged, except those the precedence table changes, each
    named in the commit message of step 1 with its row.
- **Measured.** The S21 read-by-key alone and beside a writer and the S16 indexed writes
  do not fall against the exact parent, inside the same-build band. That
  `PositionClock.Publish` takes no lock when no wait is registered is checked by the
  review.
- **Breaker targets, in order.** Close racing each kind of wait at registration and at
  publication; a fault between a cancellation and its observation; Lost on a child while
  a forwarded write waits.
- **Out of scope.** The durable states themselves (S27–S29); diagnostics of the state
  (S39).
