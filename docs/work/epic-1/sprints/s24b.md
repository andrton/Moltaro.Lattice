# S24B · Budget accounting by structure

**Track:** full, critical (the lifetime of memory) · **Needs:** S24R · **Owners:** E03, E08 ·
**Decided by:** the owner, 27 November 2026, in the planner's session

**The owner's decision.** After the outside reviews of 27 November 2026 (the architect's
and the engineering review, [refactoring backlog](../../refactoring.md#reviews-after-s24r-27-november-2026))
the owner was asked: "Перевести бюджет памяти на стандартную модель учёта до S25?"
("Move the memory budget to the standard accounting model before S25?") and answered
"ДА" ("YES"), with the example given to him: a batch that grew the text table from
2,048 to 4,096 buckets while it was prepared and was then refused keeps the 4,096
buckets, charged and reusable; its data is not published. This replaces the sentences
of the [S24 acceptance](s24.md) and its contract boundaries, and of items 1 and 2 of the
[S24A acceptance](s24a.md), that demand byte-exact charges of small managed objects, the
charge of batch input at the actual capacity of each allocation, and the return of
capacity a failed preparation grew. The decision record
[Q14](../../../decisions/write-admission.md) leaves exact managed-overhead accounting to
engineering and says the budget is not an exact cap of process memory; it asks that a
failed attempt release its partial preparation and reservations, which rule 3 keeps. Everything else S24, S24A and S24R guarantee stays.

**Why.** Byte-exact charging and exact restoration cost S24 about 7,000 shipped lines,
ten separate mechanisms that keep a smaller predecessor and reinstate it, accessors
that read and rewrite private fields of `List<T>` and `Queue<T>`, a thread-bound undo
journal inside the budget, a nullable budget the product never leaves null, and on the
ordinary write path a charged root image allocated per write and several entries of one
global lock. The measured cost against pre-S24 is 120 → 824 B per pair of ordinary
Windows writes and 6–11% on the ARM64 indexed and mixed write profiles, with no single
demonstrated cause ([S24A remaining cost](s24a.md#remaining-cost-and-structure)).

- **Goal.** The memory budget keeps its guarantee — a write that does not fit is refused
  before it has any effect, a child at the budget pauses and keeps its reads — while it
  charges memory by structure: native grants and growing managed arrays exactly, small
  fixed bookkeeping by a headroom, batch input by an estimate. An ordinary write that
  does not grow a structure does no budget work beyond one atomic reservation and
  release, and allocates what it allocated before S24.
- **Contract.** None public. The internal types replace `MemoryBudget`, `MemoryLease`,
  `BudgetSet`, `BudgetCollections`, `BudgetDictionaries`, `BudgetHashSets`,
  `BudgetBackings`, `BackingHistory` and `WorkspaceCapacity`; they are named in step 1,
  after the study. What the batch builder of the contract assembly needs stays there;
  everything else of the budget moves into the engine.
- **To read.** [Q14](../../../decisions/write-admission.md) (13 November 2026, with the
  owner's additions of 15 and 16 November 2026);
  [engine contract: initial bounded settings](../../../design/milestone-1-engine-contract.md#initial-bounded-settings);
  the [S24](s24.md), [S24A](s24a.md) and [S24R](s24r.md) records; the
  [reviews of 27 November 2026](../../refactoring.md#reviews-after-s24r-27-november-2026).

**The accounting rule.**

1. A native segment is charged at its grant, with its managed metadata, as today, and
   released at its return.
2. A managed structure that grows with the data — root pages, the primary index tables,
   overflow buckets, secondary index nodes, the arrays of the text table, collection
   tails, the retirement queue, publication scratch, delivery and connection buffers, a
   child's key set — is charged by the bytes of its backing array when it grows: reserve
   before the new array is allocated, release when the old one is dropped or, for a
   generation a reader still holds, when it is retired.
3. A refused or cancelled preparation returns the reservations it did not use and every
   slot, handle and block it prepared, and leaves installed data, revisions and position
   unchanged. Capacity that a structure grew during the attempt stays with the structure,
   charged and reusable by the next write. A native segment that the unwind leaves empty
   is released as any empty segment is.
4. Small fixed bookkeeping — owner objects of the structures, weak references, fixed
   directories, per-type and per-index headers — is not charged object by object. It is covered by a
   headroom computed when the instance opens from the schema and the options, reported
   as its own figure. The sprint measures what it covers and states the figure beside
   the constants. Objects whose number grows with what the application submits — a
   batch builder, a prepared update, a child transaction — are not in the headroom:
   they are charged by rule 5 or as the workspace of rule 2.
5. The input of a batch is charged by a deterministic estimate when it is added: its
   encoded length, a per-member constant and a per-builder constant, each measured once
   and stated beside the constant. An addition makes every check it makes today and
   builds its snapshot, in today's order — an update validates its copied content before
   its other checks, as its documentation says — and reserves the estimate last; a
   refusal by memory therefore never comes before one of those errors. A refused reservation
   reports `WriteRejected` with `Memory` synchronously, drops the snapshot, keeps the
   earlier members and leaves the addition available to retry. Building the snapshot
   before charging it is the one acquisition before reservation: its size is known only
   once it is built, and it is bounded by the caller's own input. One finalizable owner
   per batch builder returns the charge of an abandoned builder.
6. The root image of an ordinary write is one workspace owned by the arena, charged once
   at its size and grown under the writes lock when a wider image is needed. A write
   whose image fits allocates no image.
7. Reservation and release are an atomic counter. A lock, if one is needed, is taken only
   by a waiter for memory and by a release that has a waiter to wake.
8. The budget is never null; an instance or a standalone structure in a test that has no
   limit uses an unlimited budget.
9. No shipped code reads or writes non-public members of runtime types, mirrors their
   private layouts or calls their internal helpers. The retirement queue and the lists of
   batch operations use the engine's own arrays. The capacity of a .NET collection is
   read only through its public members (to be verified in the sprint for each type used,
   by the rule on platform calls).

- **Acceptance.**
  - A write whose reservation does not fit is refused with `WriteRejected` and `Memory`
    before commit and before any effect; reads continue; after memory is freed the same
    write is accepted. A slot that cannot be addressed is still `RootSlots`. A complete
    batch's refusal has no `OperationIndex`.
  - Boundaries, each derived in the test from the accounting rule and the layout, not from
    the code under test, at one byte below, exactly at and one byte above: the next native
    grant with its metadata and the headroom; the growth of a primary index table; the
    growth of a text-table array; a batch addition at its estimate, for a full value and
    for `AddUpdate`, on the master and on a child, the child's refusal sending no request.
  - A refused write that grew a structure, followed by a write that needs the same
    growth, takes no new charge for it; the refused write's data is not visible to a read
    by key, by index or by enumeration.
  - The charge is never below what the engine holds: an independent inventory in the
    test project — native grants with their metadata and the managed backing arrays of the structures of
    rule 2, read through named internal inspection members — is at most the charge, and
    the charge exceeds it by at most the headroom and the batch estimates in flight. This
    holds after load, during growth with a reader holding the previous generation, after
    refusals and cancellations, and after the churn trace of S24.
  - After close the charge is zero and every native grant is returned.
  - Two batch builders on application threads and the publishing thread competing for the
    last bytes never admit more than the limit.
  - An abandoned batch builder's charge returns after collection, held by a test with a
    weak reference.
  - A Ready child at the budget pauses application at the first blocked change, keeps its
    reads at its position, and resumes in order, once, when memory is freed — also when
    the memory is freed between the refusal and the registration of its wait, without
    waiting for a retry pause. Before Ready a child that cannot hold the state ends open
    with `Unavailable`. Close and cancellation end the wait; nothing queued runs after
    its cancellation.
  - No member of the shipped assemblies carries an `UnsafeAccessor` aimed at a type
    outside them; a test states it. That no call path uses reflection is checked by the
    review.
  - **The tests that change.** Step 1 replaces exactly the assertions of S24, S24A and
    S24R that state what this decision removes: (a) byte-exact charges of the small
    bookkeeping of rule 4; (b) any capacity a refused or cancelled attempt grew and gave
    back within the call — native segments, workspaces, the retirement queue, text
    metadata, and every predecessor the ten mechanisms of the
    [reviews](../../refactoring.md#reviews-after-s24r-27-november-2026) keep and reinstate;
    (c) the withdrawal of a never-published tail page and the transfer of its generation
    history; (d) a reservation boundary between two fields of one addition ("later
    getters remain unread"); (e) reads of private fields by name; (f) the product-side
    inventory switch; (g) batch input charged at the actual capacity of each allocation,
    reserved before acquisition, and the place of that reservation before the duplicate,
    submitted and closed checks. The commit message of step 1 lists
    each replaced test under its letter. Every other assertion of those sprints stays as
    it is; the reviewer checks that each change falls under a letter.
- **Measured.** The criterion is fixed before the results are read. Three exact
  revisions on one matching host each, paired and rotated as in S24R: pre-S24
  `dd519ba4`, S24R `2d8ff204`, the candidate, with a separately built twin of the
  candidate for the same-build band.
  - Windows workstation, the public pair of S24A and the S14 controls: allocation per
    pair back to the pre-S24 figure of the same workload, within 16 B.
  - Rented ARM64: the S16 indexed writes (changed and unchanged key) and the S23 mixed
    `Replace`: the candidate against pre-S24 inside the same-build band. A remainder
    outside it does not close the sprint: its cause is looked for by the three steps of
    [when to stop and ask the owner](../../process.md#when-to-stop-and-ask-the-owner),
    starting with a diagnostic build of the candidate whose budget does no accounting at
    all (not a product mode), which bounds the cost of the budget itself.
  - Reads by key and protected views (S21) unchanged within the band.
  - The cold batch of S24A at 1,024, 2,048 and 4,096 dictionary texts: each doubling
    takes fewer than three times the chain visits, as S24A required; relinks and parked
    visits are reported on the three revisions.
  - Reported, not judged: S20 partial updates on the three revisions, the first figure
    after S24; the share of the budget of each counted part on the reference fixture, as
    S24 reported it, the headroom, and the peak against steady of the churn trace;
    shipped lines, callable bodies and branching points against S24R.
- **Study.** Before step 1, bounded, read-only, at pinned commits, the licence checked
  first: how DataFusion's `MemoryPool` and `MemoryReservation`, RocksDB's
  `WriteBufferManager` with its cache reservation, and Garnet's size tracking of its
  object store charge memory — at what granularity, what a failed operation returns, how
  headroom is set, and how reservation avoids a lock — and which public members of
  `List<T>`, `Dictionary<TKey,TValue>`, `HashSet<T>` and `Queue<T>` report capacity in
  .NET 10. The report says what Lattice takes and why.
- **Breaker targets, in order.** A release racing a waiter's registration (no missed and
  no double wake); a refusal at every reservation point of a batch and of a child
  transaction, the ledger checked after close; the charge against the inventory while a
  reader holds an old generation; concurrent builders at the last bytes; an abandoned
  builder.
- **Documents.** The paragraph on accounting of the
  [engine contract](../../../design/milestone-1-engine-contract.md#initial-bounded-settings)
  says the rule above; a section in
  [storage and maintenance](../../../design/storage-and-maintenance.md) describes the

- **Out of scope.** One lifecycle of the instance (S25L). The duplication between the

**Small change during the sprint.** Rule 5 named "schema" among the checks made before
the snapshot is built, while some schema checks, such as a required field with no value,
run while it is built. The review of the brief asked for "every check it makes today that
needs no snapshot"; the planner gave the wording to this sprint. The review of the
internal contract found that this order would move the content check of an update after
its identity checks, against the documentation of `ILatticeWriteBatch`, so the rule keeps
today's order of the checks and of the snapshot. The guarantee is unchanged: the
reservation comes last.

## Source study

Each licence was checked at its pinned commit before the code was read. Lattice takes
ideas only; no code is adapted.

- **Apache DataFusion** at `273d37a5`, Apache-2.0. Large consumers hold their own
  reservation, sized from capacity
  ([proxy.rs](https://github.com/apache/datafusion/blob/273d37a5968571900bfe9efa1ee89f97914da118/datafusion/common/src/utils/proxy.rs#L91-L111));
  small allocations are left to a margin the user sets
  ([mod.rs](https://github.com/apache/datafusion/blob/273d37a5968571900bfe9efa1ee89f97914da118/datafusion/execution/src/memory_pool/mod.rs#L44-L51)).
  The greedy pool is one atomic updated by compare-and-update; a refused `try_grow`
  writes nothing
  ([pool.rs](https://github.com/apache/datafusion/blob/273d37a5968571900bfe9efa1ee89f97914da118/datafusion/execution/src/memory_pool/pool.rs#L81-L104)).
  The default pool is an unlimited object, never an absent one. Taken: atomic admission,
  a refusal that leaves nothing, charging by capacity, an unlimited default. Not taken:
  spilling, charging after growth, copying a dependency's private layout.
- **RocksDB** at `a6197a4b`, read under the Apache-2.0 option of its dual licence. Memtable
  memory is charged per arena block at the granted size; a write that fits its block does
  no accounting, and what a write consumed stays charged until the memtable is freed
  ([arena.cc](https://github.com/facebook/rocksdb/blob/a6197a4b630902b1e529840411ecfa7eb71a189c/memory/arena.cc#L145-L168)).
  A stalled writer registers and then checks again, signalling itself if the stall
  already ended
  ([write_buffer_manager.cc](https://github.com/facebook/rocksdb/blob/a6197a4b630902b1e529840411ecfa7eb71a189c/memtable/write_buffer_manager.cc#L809-L855));
  a release below the limit locks whether or not anyone waits. Taken: charging per block
  or array at its granted size, grown capacity that stays charged, a check after
  registration, a manager that is never absent. Not taken: the soft limit, absorbed
  failures, the unconditional lock.
- **Microsoft Garnet** at `ff769f86`, MIT. Heap objects are charged by an estimate, length
  rounded up to 8 plus constants
  ([MemoryUtils.cs](https://github.com/microsoft/garnet/blob/ff769f86e0ca316e7308d14682ac4e648c1de05c/libs/storage/Tsavorite/cs/src/core/Utilities/MemoryUtils.cs#L13-L44)),
  with `Interlocked.Add` and no lock; its wake publishes, then reads the flag
  ([LogSizeTracker.cs](https://github.com/microsoft/garnet/blob/ff769f86e0ca316e7308d14682ac4e648c1de05c/libs/storage/Tsavorite/cs/src/core/Index/Common/LogSizeTracker.cs#L297-L472)).
  Taken: an estimate of length plus constants for caller input, the interlocked counter,
  that order of a wake. Not taken: charging afterwards, eviction, a target band.
- **.NET 10**, from the documentation, the source at dotnet/dotnet `8e0f8ed2` (MIT) and a
  program run on .NET 10.0.15, x64. `List<T>.Capacity` is the length of its one array and
  its setter allocates exactly that length. `Queue<T>`, `Dictionary<TKey,TValue>` and
  `HashSet<T>` report `Capacity` (since .NET 9), but a dictionary or a set holds two
  private arrays at a prime chosen by an internal helper, so public members give neither
  its bytes per slot nor its next capacity before it grows. An array occupies a 24-byte
  header plus its elements, rounded up to 8, on a 64-bit runtime. Not run on ARM64.

The sources support the rules as written; none of them gives a refusal before any effect
by itself. Rule 7 takes the order of RocksDB and Garnet: the waiter marks itself, then
tries again; a release changes the counter, then reads the mark, and locks only when it
is set. No structure of rule 2 keeps a .NET dictionary, set or queue.

## Internal contract

Written by the coordinator after the study, from a read-only survey of the code at
`758f1c5f`. A name or a signature the implementer needs and does not find here goes back
to the coordinator.

**Contract assembly.** The folder `Budgeting` of `Moltaro.Lattice` is removed; the batch
builder reaches the budget through the boundary it already has.

- `Access/Batches/IWriteBatchExecutor` gains `bool TryReserveInput(long bytes)`, false when
  the instance cannot hold the bytes, and `void ReleaseInput(long bytes)`. `CheckUpdate`
  returns the encoded length of the update as a `long`. The `EncodeRoot` overload that
  takes a lease is removed. `ExecuteAsync` takes `ArraySegment<WriteBatchOperation>`.
- `Access/Batches/BatchInputCharge` (new, sealed, finalizable): the one owner of a
  builder's charge. It holds the executor and the charged bytes, never the builder.
  `const long PerBuilderBytes` and `const long PerMemberBytes`, each with the figure it
  measured beside it; `bool TryAdd(long bytes)`; `void Dispose()` and the finalizer return
  the whole charge once.
- `WriteBatch` keeps its operations in a `WriteBatchOperation[]` with a count and its
  identities in a plain `HashSet<WriteBatchEntity>`. An addition makes its checks and
  builds its snapshot in today's order (rule 5) — the encoded root, or the checked update;
  nothing for a delete — and calls `TryAdd` with its length plus `PerMemberBytes`, plus
  `PerBuilderBytes` on the first addition; the charge object exists before that first
  reservation. On false it
  throws `WriteRejected` with `Memory`, or `ObjectDisposedException` when the instance
  closed meanwhile, drops the snapshot and consumes no update. Commit hands the charge to
  the execution, which releases it when the batch ends.
- The lease parameters that S24 added to the binding and value types (`ClassBinder`,
  `BoundProperty`, `EntityBinding`, `RecordValues`, `FieldValues`, `LatticeRecord`,
  `LatticeOwnedValue`, `LatticeUpdate`, `UpdateInput`, `UpdateValues`, `SequenceForm`,
  `MapForm`) are removed. No public signature changes. The XML documentation of
  `ILatticeWriteBatch` that says an addition "stops at that allocation, before taking any
  later input" says instead that it reserves after its input is copied (letter d).

**Engine.** A new folder `Storage/Budgeting`, namespace `Moltaro.Lattice.Storage.Budgeting`.

- `MemoryBudget` (sealed): one per instance, never null; `new MemoryBudget()` is unlimited
  and serves a standalone structure in a test.
  - `MemoryBudget(long limit = long.MaxValue)`, `void Open(long limit)`, `long Limit`,
    `long Charged`, `long Peak`, `void ResetPeak()`, `long Headroom`.
  - `bool TryReserve(long bytes)`: a compare-and-swap loop on the charge, never past the
    limit, no lock. `void Reserve(long bytes)` throws `BudgetRefusedException` with
    `Memory`. `void Release(long bytes)`: an interlocked subtraction, then the wake below.
  - `static long ArrayBytes<T>(int length)`: the bytes of a `T[length]`, the 24-byte header
    stated beside it and asserted by a test against `GC.GetAllocatedBytesForCurrentThread`.
  - Rule 2: `T[] Allocate<T>(int length)` reserves, then allocates; `void Release<T>(T[]
    array)`; `void Grow<T>(ref T[] array, int length)` reserves the new array, allocates,
    copies, publishes it and releases the old one, for a structure no reader holds;
    `void EnsureCapacity<T>(List<T> list, int count)` reserves the next capacity, sets
    `List<T>.Capacity` and releases the old one. A structure whose old generation a reader
    may hold allocates the new one and gives the old one with its bytes to
    `RetiredMemory`; they are released when that retirement is freed.
  - `void ReserveHeadroom(long bytes)`: reserves the headroom once and makes it the figure
    `Headroom`.
  - The wait of a child (rule 7), one waiter per instance: `long BeginRetry()` sets the
    waiter's flag and the thread of its attempt with an interlocked write and returns a
    ticket, the count of frees so far; `void EndRetry()` clears the thread; `Task
    WhenFreedAfter(long ticket)` completes at the first free counted after the ticket, or
    at close; `void EndWait()` clears the flag; `void NoteReusable()` is called where a
    native or a root slot becomes reusable. A release or a reusable space reads the flag
    and does nothing more when it is clear or when it comes from the thread of the
    attempt; otherwise it takes the lock, counts the free and completes the signal. A
    refused follower raises `ObserveChildPressure` after the rollback of the refused
    attempt, then calls `BeginRetry`, attempts again, calls `EndRetry` in a `finally`,
    and when refused again awaits `WhenFreedAfter(ticket)` with the retry pause as its
    timeout and its token; `EndWait` runs in a `finally` when the wait ends. A free made
    in `ObserveChildPressure` therefore comes before `BeginRetry`, and the change is
    applied by the second attempt; a free made while the follower waits wakes it, and the
    third attempt applies it. The two
    marks answer two races: memory freed before `BeginRetry` is seen by the attempt that
    follows it, and memory freed after it moves the count past the ticket; the thread
    keeps the attempt's own rollback from counting as a free, which would wake the
    follower at once and again.
  - `void Close()` ends admission, completes the wait and clears the charge; a release
    after close changes nothing.
- `BudgetRefusedException` moves here unchanged.
- `Headroom` (static): `long For(LatticeSchema schema, LatticeDatabaseOptions options)` = a
  fixed part of the instance + entity types × a part per type + (entity types + secondary
  indexes) × a part per index, plus the fixed part of a child or of a listener when the

- `Storage/Hashing/ChargedSet<T>` (sealed): `BudgetSet<T>` moved into the engine without

  `Clear`, `ReportHeld`. It is the one engine-owned hash set: the key set of
  `PublicationKeys`, which a child keeps between its transactions, and the index of
  `CollectionTails` by root handle use it.
- `Arena.RootImage(int length)` (rule 6), under the writes lock: the arena's one root image
  of at least `length` bytes, grown by `Grow` when it is short. `RootWriter` writes into it
  and returns nothing at its end. The export of a batch member writes into a buffer of its
  own, as before S24, covered by rule 5.

**Write units.** An ordinary write, a child transaction and a forwarded request on the
master each reserve one estimate when they start — `PerMemberBytes` for each member, plus
the encoded length of a partial update's commands — and release it once when they end;
the owner keeps it in a `long`. No other input is charged again: the values of a whole
write stay the caller's until it runs and are encoded into the root image and the native
blocks, which rules 6 and 1 charge, and the body of a child transaction or of a forwarded
request stays in the connection buffers that carry it, which rule 2 charges. Their
working objects (edits, claims, decoded commands and keys, replies) are plain allocations
under that estimate. The members of a batch are charged by its builder and take no second
estimate. `PerMemberBytes` is the largest per-member figure measured on the reference
fixture for a batch member and for a write unit; a test measures each constant on the
running runtime and fails when it is below.

**What a refused or cancelled preparation returns (rule 3).** Its reservations and its
estimate; its native blocks and root slots to their free lists and its root handles; the
texts it added; its index reservations, retirement places and collection-tail loans.
Arrays it grew stay with their structures. A segment it leaves empty goes to the reserve
or is released, as any empty segment. The ten mechanisms of the reviews are removed:
`WorkspaceCapacity` with `BackingHistory` and the chain of workspace users; the journal of
the budget and its brackets on both roles; the smaller segment directory and
`ReturnWhenEmpty` of `Arena`; the withdrawal, smaller directories and generation history
of `RootTable`, with `CaptureExtent`; the smaller directory of `OverflowBuckets`; the
previous tables and reserved hashes of `PrimaryIndex` and `IndexTable`; the earlier
buckets, deferred work and parked entries of `TextTable`, which again does its bounded
maintenance on a miss, during a preparation too; the earlier leaves of `TextLocator`; the
earlier first block of `TextWriterSlots`; the borrow history of `CollectionTails`. The
test hooks that serve them, `BeforePreparedTextBucketGrowth` among them, go with them.

**Rule 9.** `CollectionTails` keeps its persistent records in its own arrays, a record
array linked per root, found through a `ChargedSet<T>` by root handle; its maps of one
preparation stay plain collections under the estimate of the write. `UpdateCommands`
and the identities of a batch use a plain `HashSet<T>` as working state. A `List<T>` that
a structure keeps is charged by `Capacity` through `EnsureCapacity`.

**Close and the balance.** Close ends admission, completes the wait, releases the native
grants as they are returned and clears the charge; a builder disposed or collected after
close changes nothing. The balance the reviews asked to see checked is checked by the
inventory of the tests (the planner, on `71b44cee`). At a point of rest — no write, no
child transaction, no forwarded request and no batch builder alive — the charge equals
the native grants with their metadata, plus the held arrays sized by the tests' own rule,
plus `Headroom`: an equality, not a bound, since rules 1, 2 and 4 charge each term
exactly, so a leak of any size shows. The bound of the Acceptance, with the estimates in
flight, holds where a builder or a write is alive. The test of close states the equality
as its last assertion before close, then that every native grant was returned. A term
that cannot be made exact is named here, beside the inventory; the bound is not widened

## Implementation and review

**Tests changed after the gate.** Eleven groups of tests of step 1 and of earlier sprints
were wrong against this brief, and the coordinator decided each. `f51cb682` lists them with
their reasons: an accessor of the replaced retirement queue, limits and rooms sized against
S24's charging, replays that ignored rule 2's copy peaks, the pre-S24 resolution and text

rule 3, a trace mode the program never builds, and two measurements that raced. Later
setup changes on the coordinator's decision: `f52e2c36` and `c58a6eb4` (the headroom test
measures the instance alone, on schemas without a text table), `df7f5f13` (a child
transaction reserves its estimate).

**Decisions during review.** Each is carried by the code and the documents named.

- A child's batch returns its builder charge at its master's reply, not after its own
  application: `IWriteBatchExecutor.ExecuteAsync` takes the `BatchInputCharge`.
- Every write unit reserves `BatchInputCharge.PerUnitBytes` (2,048 B) beside
  `PerMemberBytes` for each member and its lengths: an ordinary write at its first
  preparation, a child transaction when it starts, a forwarded request on the master when
  its handling starts, a write a child forwards before it is encoded, a batch in its
  builder's first charge. The figure is the largest one-member unit measured over both
  nodes, a forwarded batch of one, 2,579 B and 1,299 B above its member (the planner's
  rule). A write or a batch that a child forwards also reserves the encoded length of its
  request after encoding and before sending; a refusal sends nothing. This replaces the
  paragraph "Write units" of the internal contract where it says that a unit reserves
  `PerMemberBytes` alone and that the body of a forwarded request is charged only in the
  connection buffers.
- A child transaction reserves its estimate from the number of effects that the
  transaction header now carries; a number the length cannot carry, or one that disagrees
  with the effects, is a malformed transaction.
- A batch's key set, on a master and for a forwarded batch, is a working object under the
  estimate of its batch (rule 5 and the write units), a plain `HashSet<T>`; the child's set
  stays a `ChargedSet<T>` charged to the child.
- The one unlimited budget in shipped code is `TransferBuffer()`, for the export of a batch
  member above 64 KiB: its bytes are the member's snapshot, which the builder's estimate
  charges (rule 5), and its pieces keep it off the large object heap.
- A replaced directory a reader may hold — `Arena._segments`, `RootTable._pages`,
  `OverflowBuckets._pages` — stays charged until no read of its epoch remains
  (`MemoryBudget.ReleaseAfterReads`, released by `RetiredMemory`, the rest at close).
- A replaced text leaf and a dropped branch are released at once. The headroom holds one
  largest leaf and one branch per read slot (the planner): the worst case reads can hold,
  1,176 B a slot. A host has at least 128 slots, so the allowance is 150,528 B on every
  host up to 64 logical processors, 3.6% of a 4 MiB limit, and grows with the slots above
  that. Keeping a leaf charged until its readers leave would put a retirement on
  every write that changes a text; that is measured only if a real limit finds the
  allowance too large.
- The headroom is computed from the schema, the options and the read slots of the host
  (`ReadEpochs.HostSlotCount`); `Headroom` lives in `Engine` beside `EngineDatabase`, so
  that it reads the layout of the text table.
